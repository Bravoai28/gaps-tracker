# GAPS diet research notes

These are short, paraphrased notes compiled to build the food lists in this app.
They are **not** a substitute for the book *Gut and Psychology Syndrome* by
Dr Natasha Campbell-McBride, and they are **not medical advice**. Every child is
different; stage timing and food choices should be agreed with a qualified GAPS
practitioner and the child's doctor/dietitian. No text from the book is
reproduced here.

1. GAPS Diet (official US site), *Introduction Diet* page – https://www.gapsdiet.com/introduction-diet/
2. GAPS Diet, *Introduction Diet quick resource guide* (PDF) – https://www.gapsdiet.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Introduction-Diet.pdf
3. GAPS Diet Australia, *The Full GAPS Diet* (republishes an outline credited to Dr Campbell-McBride) – https://gapsaustralia.com.au/full-gaps-diet/
4. GAPS Diet, *Full GAPS Diet* page and PDF – https://www.gapsdiet.com/full-gaps-diet/ and https://www.gapsdiet.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/FullGAPSDiet-1.pdf
5. GAPS Info, *GAPS Introduction Diet* – http://www.gapsinfo.com/gaps-introduction-diet/

A note on evidence: the independent reviews (6, 7) point out that the GAPS
protocol has not been tested in controlled clinical trials and that very
restrictive diets can carry nutritional risks for children, which is another
reason to keep a practitioner involved.

## General principles (all stages)

- **Build up one food at a time, starting tiny.** A new food starts at a
  teaspoon or so and is increased over several days only if it is tolerated
  (1, 3, 5).
- **Watch for reactions.** The main signal used is the stool – a return of
  diarrhoea, tummy pain, bloating or constipation after a new food suggests the
  child is not ready for it. The usual advice is to stop that food, wait about a
  week, and try again (3, 6). Skin flare-ups, sleep disruption and behaviour
  changes are also worth noting, as reactions can be delayed (4).
- **Moving on.** There is no fixed timetable. Some families pass through a
  stage in a day or two, others stay for weeks. The general rule is to move on
  when the foods of the current stage are well tolerated and stools are
  settling (3, 6). Foods can be introduced later than the stage suggests if the
  child is sensitive (3).
- **Sensitivity (skin) test.** For a food that might cause an allergic
  reaction (dairy and eggs are the usual examples), the sources describe
  dabbing a little of the food on the inside of the wrist at bedtime and
  checking it in the morning; an angry red patch means postpone that food for a
  few weeks (3).
- **Probiotic foods are introduced gradually from stage 1** – roughly 1–2
  teaspoons a day for a few days, then a little more, until a spoonful can be
  stirred into each cup of stock or bowl of soup. Add them once the food has
  cooled a little, as heat kills the bacteria (3).
- **Stock and soup stay central** throughout the Introduction Diet and should
  continue at least daily afterwards (3).
- **Plenty of natural fat** (from the stock, meat, and later ghee/butter/olive
  oil) is encouraged with meals (3, 4).
- **Throughout:** no grains, sugar, starchy vegetables (potato, sweet potato,
  parsnip, yam), starch powders, soy, processed/packaged/tinned food, or stock
  cubes (3, 4, 7).

## Stage 1 – the starting point (the child is here now)

**Allowed (staples):**
- **Homemade meat or fish stock**, made by gently simmering bones, joints and
  meat on the bone (chicken is a gentle place to start), with salt and a few
  peppercorns. Warm stock is offered as a drink with and between meals, and the
  fat is kept in (1, 3).
- **Boiled meats and fish**, plus the soft gelatinous tissue and bone marrow
  taken off the bones; boiled liver/organ meats are included in some guides (2, 3).
- **Homemade soup** made with that stock and **well-cooked, non-fibrous
  vegetables** – for example carrot, onion, leek, courgette, marrow, squash and
  pumpkin (peeled and seeded), and the florets of cauliflower and broccoli
  (stalks removed). Vegetables are cooked until very soft; the soup can be
  blended. A little garlic can be added at the end (3).
- **Animal fats** from the stock and meat; one guide also lists coconut oil (2).
- **Probiotic juices** – the liquid from homemade sauerkraut or fermented
  vegetables, starting from a teaspoon or two a day (3).
- **Homemade yoghurt / kefir (or whey dripped from yoghurt)** – only if dairy
  is tolerated (after a sensitivity test), and introduced very slowly (2, 3).
- **Drinks:** warm stock, **ginger tea**, **chamomile** or mint tea, a little
  honey in tea, and warm filtered water (1, 2, 3).

**Avoid at stage 1:** everything not on the list above – including eggs,
raw vegetables, fibrous vegetables (cabbage family, celery), fruit, nuts,
roasted/fried meat, olive oil, ghee/butter, spices, and all of the
"throughout" exclusions.

## Stage 2

**Add (one at a time):** raw organic egg yolks stirred into soup/stock, then
soft-boiled eggs; stews and casseroles made with meat, vegetables, salt and
fresh herbs (no spices yet); homemade fermented fish; homemade ghee starting
from a teaspoon; more of the probiotic foods (yoghurt/kefir if introduced, more
fermented-vegetable juice). Some guides list cod liver oil here (2, 3, 5).

**Still avoid:** fried/scrambled eggs, avocado, nuts, solid sauerkraut,
roasted meat, olive oil, raw vegetables, fruit, spices.

## Stage 3

**Add:** ripe avocado mashed into soup (from a teaspoon or so); small pancakes
made from nut butter, egg and blended squash/courgette, fried gently in fat;
scrambled or gently fried eggs with plenty of fat; onion slow-cooked in fat;
the fermented vegetables / sauerkraut themselves (not just the juice),
building up gradually. One guide also adds more fibrous cooked vegetables such
as cabbage, celeriac and asparagus here (2, 3, 5).

**Still avoid:** roasted/grilled meat, olive oil, juices, nut-flour bread,
raw vegetables, fruit.

## Stage 4

**Add:** roasted and grilled meats (not barbecued or fried, no charred bits);
cold-pressed olive oil starting from a few drops; freshly pressed juice
starting with a few spoonfuls of carrot juice, later with celery, lettuce and
mint, ideally on an empty stomach; bread baked from ground nuts or seeds
(starting with a small piece) (2, 3, 5).

**Still avoid:** raw vegetables, fruit (other than later in juices), sweet
baking.

## Stage 5

**Add:** cooked apple purée (peeled, stewed, mashed with some fat), starting
with a few spoonfuls; raw vegetables, beginning with soft lettuce and peeled
cucumber and then others such as carrot, tomato, onion and cabbage; fruit added
to vegetable juice (apple, pineapple, mango). **Citrus is still avoided** (3, 5).

## Stage 6

**Add:** peeled raw apple, then other raw fruit, more honey, and sweet baking
made with allowed ingredients (dried fruit can be used as a sweetener) (3, 5).

## Full GAPS diet

Usually entered once the Introduction Diet is complete and stools are more or
less normal (3).

**Built around:** meat, fish, shellfish, eggs, stock and soups, fermented
dairy (if tolerated), fermented vegetables, and lots of vegetables (cooked,
raw and fermented), with natural fats (butter, ghee, animal fats, coconut,
olive oil). Around 85% of daily food is meant to be savoury; fruit, nuts and
nut-flour baking are snacks in moderation, and fruit is best eaten apart from
meals (3, 4). Fermented dairy such as butter and natural hard cheese is part
of the full diet when tolerated; properly soaked white (navy) beans are
mentioned as allowed in one review (4, 7).

**Avoid on Full GAPS:** all grains (wheat, rice, oats, corn, etc.) and
grain-like seeds (quinoa, buckwheat, millet, amaranth), sugar and syrups,
potatoes, sweet potatoes, parsnips, yams, starch powders (tapioca, arrowroot,
cornflour), soy, fresh/pasteurised milk, processed and tinned foods, and
additives (3, 4, 7).

**Leaving GAPS:** typically after 1.5–2 years, and only after stools have been
normal for a sustained period. New potatoes and fermented gluten-free grains
are tried first in small amounts; wheat, sugar and processed foods are kept out
the longest (3, 6).

**If a stomach bug or diarrhoea returns:** step back to the gentle,
low-fibre stage-1 style foods for a few days until stools normalise, then
reintroduce raw vegetables, nuts and fruit gradually again (3).
